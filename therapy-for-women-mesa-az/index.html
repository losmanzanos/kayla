Therapy for eldest daughters, people-pleasers, and over-functioners, with support for maternal burnout. Mesa, AZ and virtual across Arizona.

You're Allowed to Need Things

You've been strong for everyone else.
It's your turn.

Maybe you were the responsible one growing up. The one who read the room, smoothed things over, and made sure nobody was upset. You got very good at it. So good that now you're not sure what you want when no one needs anything from you.

If you're exhausted in a way sleep doesn't fix, if saying no makes your chest tight, if you'd rather do it yourself than ask, you're in the right place.

This is therapy for women who carry a lot. Some are mothers, many aren't. What they share is a habit of putting themselves last, and a quiet sense that it's time to stop.

What We Can Work On

This space was built for you

Eldest Daughter Syndrome

If you grew up as the responsible one, the caretaker, the emotional anchor of your family, those patterns didn't go away when you grew up. We can trace them, understand them, and create something different.

People-Pleasing & Fawning

The chronic tendency to prioritize everyone else's comfort at the expense of your own. We'll work with the parts of you that learned this was the safest way to be and help them find new strategies.

Perfectionism & Over-Functioning

Doing everything, doing it well, and doing it before anyone has to ask. From the outside it looks like competence. From the inside it often feels like fear. We slow it down and look at what it's protecting.

Losing Yourself in Your Roles

Daughter, partner, friend, employee, mother. When every role is about someone else, it's easy to lose track of who you are underneath them. Therapy helps you find her again.

Generational Patterns

A lot of what you carry was handed to you, and a lot of it was handed to the women before you. Naming those patterns and choosing what to keep is some of the most meaningful work we do.

Maternal Burnout & Postpartum

If you're also a mother, the load gets heavier. Burnout, postpartum anxiety, and depression are common and treatable. More on that below.

The Approach

How we work
together

Most of the women I work with came to therapy reluctantly. They fit the appointment between a work deadline and someone else's needs. They felt guilty taking an hour for themselves. Some cried in the parking lot before the first session.

That's exactly where we start. Not with a treatment plan, but with being seen.

IFS for the Parts That Keep Everyone Okay

Internal Family Systems helps us get to know the parts of you that have been working overtime: the inner critic that says you're not doing enough, the people-pleaser who says yes before you've had a chance to think, the exhausted part who just wants to disappear for a weekend. When those parts feel understood rather than managed, they naturally start to relax.

Healing the Eldest Daughter

Eldest daughter syndrome is a pattern, not a diagnosis, but its impact is real. It often looks like chronic anxiety, difficulty receiving help, extreme responsibility, resentment that feels shameful, and a deep sense that your needs are a burden. These patterns formed for a reason. In therapy, we work to honor that reason while freeing you from it.

Unlearning the Fawn Response

Fawning is a survival strategy. If keeping other people happy was how you stayed safe, your nervous system learned it well. We work gently with your body and your parts so that having a preference, or a boundary, stops feeling like a threat.

Good fit for you if:

  • You identify as an eldest or parentified daughter
  • You say yes when you mean no
  • You can't stop over-functioning
  • You feel guilty for having needs
  • You've lost your sense of self in your roles
  • You want to break generational cycles
  • You're a mother running on empty
  • You're ready to put yourself on the list

For Mothers

Maternal burnout & postpartum support

Many of the women I see are mothers, and motherhood has a way of turning the volume up on everything above. The bone-deep exhaustion that rest doesn't touch. The guilt for wanting time alone. The feeling that you've disappeared into caring for everyone else.

Postpartum anxiety and depression are underdiagnosed and undertreated. If you're experiencing intrusive thoughts, rage, numbness, or a disconnect from your baby or yourself, that's not a reflection of who you are as a mother. It's a response to an enormous transition, and it responds to treatment.

Therapy here isn't about making you a better mother. It's about helping you remember that you're a whole person who deserves to be held too.

A word from Kayla

Healing doesn't mean
becoming someone else. It means coming home.

The women I work with are often the last people who would ever ask for help. They're the ones who text back immediately, who show up for everyone, who keep things running on empty and never let anyone see it.

What I want you to know is that asking for support isn't the opposite of strength. It's one of the bravest things a person can do. Especially when you've spent your whole life believing that your needs didn't matter as much as everyone else's.

You matter. This work matters. And you deserve a therapist who gets it.
